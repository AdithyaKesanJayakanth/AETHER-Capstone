## Plain text

**Where it started.** Two years ago my project was a model Formula One car. Shape it, simulate it, race it, and find out whether the numbers were right. It left me with one habit: change one thing at a time, and measure.

**ISRO.** Then I visited ISRO. In front of a re-entry capsule, the first thing you notice is how blunt it is. To someone who had spent a year chasing streamlines, it looked wrong. Blunt is the point: the flat face pushes the shock away, and that keeps the heat off the shield. I went home with a question. If the shape makes both the drag and the heating, how do you choose it?

**Designing the study.** So AETHER became a bounded experiment. One capsule family: nose radius, shoulder radius, cone angle. One condition: the Apollo wind-tunnel test NASA published in 1966, so there was real data to be judged against. Two objectives: maximise drag, minimise peak heat flux. And one rule, written before any result: the lowest heating among shapes keeping ninety-eight percent of the baseline drag.

**Building it.** I built the pipeline myself. One parameter table draws the shape, meshes it, and runs the solver. Twenty shapes, fifty-nine cases, three mesh levels, two benchmarks against NASA data.

**What broke.** And the solver fought back. The residuals converged, to a solution that was wrong: a carbuncle, a numerical shock mode with the stagnation pressure twelve percent above anything physics allows. The monitors said done. The pitot pressure said no. The fix was not a setting. It was the mesh: stretch the cells along the shock, and the instability disappears.

**The answer.** The answer. Drag follows the nose and shoulder radius; the cone angle does nothing. The heating peak lives at the shoulder. And a nose radius of one diameter gives five percent less peak heating for under two percent of drag, four times the margin I measured by refining the mesh.

**What I take with me.** What I take with me. Write the rule before the result. A converged solution is not a correct one until the physics agrees. Keep the failures; they are the evidence. The capsule at ISRO looked wrong until I understood it. Now I know what it costs to make it look right. I'm Adithya, and this is Project AETHER.
